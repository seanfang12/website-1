What I Wish I Knew When I Started Lifting

I started going to the gym consistently last year, and it ended up helping me in way more ways than I expected. When I had a rough week from exams or recruiting rejections, the gym became a place where I could be alone with my thoughts, blast my favorite music, and focus on something I could control.

I also gained a lot of confidence as I got stronger, lost body fat, and started feeling better about how I looked. The gym also became really social for me because I would run into friends, lift with people, and just hang out. I used to believe things like lifting weights would stunt your growth because that was what I heard growing up, but later I learned a lot of beginner fitness advice is either outdated or way more complicated than it needs to be. I want this site to be the kind of guide I wish I had when I started.

What Beginners Actually Need to Focus On

I think beginners overcomplicate exercise selection, rep ranges, form, and how much weight they should be using. Those things matter, but I used to think every detail had to be perfectly optimized. What matters more to me now is training hard, progressing over time, and actually staying consistent.

  • Train hard
  • Progress over time
  • Pick exercises you will actually stick with

Why Upper/Lower Works Best for Me

I like upper/lower because I can train each muscle group around twice a week while still having about three rest days. I usually train four to five times a week, so that schedule works really well for me without making me feel like I need to be in the gym every single day.

When I first started lifting, I tried a push/pull/legs split. I realized pretty quickly that it felt like too many gym days for me and not enough recovery. Upper/lower feels easier to stick to while still giving me the training frequency I want.

Why I Usually Prefer Machines and Cables

I usually prefer machines and cables because they feel more stable, especially when I am training close to failure. With something like a barbell bench press, failing a rep without a spotter can be a lot more stressful than failing on a machine where I can just stop the movement.

I genuinely prefer chest-supported T-bar rows over barbell rows and cable lateral raises over dumbbell lateral raises. That does not mean I hate free weights though. Incline dumbbell press is still one of my favorite exercises because I actually enjoy doing it. To me, a good exercise is something I enjoy, can progressively overload, and can actually feel working the muscle I am trying to grow.

Seated Cable Row

I like cable rows because they are easy to control and I can repeat basically the same movement every set.

Chest-Supported T-Bar Row

This is one of my favorite back exercises because the chest support keeps me stable and lets me focus more on my back instead of balancing the weight.

Lat Pulldown

Lat pulldowns are simple to progress because changing the weight is easy and the movement is pretty repeatable.

Hip Thrust

I like the machine version because the setup is easy and I can focus mostly on the movement instead of worrying about positioning a barbell.

Incline Dumbbell Press

Even though I use a lot of machines, incline dumbbell press is still one of my favorite exercises because it is just fun to do and gives me a good way to progress my pressing strength.

Why I Like Training Heavy

I usually like working in the 6 to 8 rep range because it feels like a good balance between using challenging weight and not doing a ton of extra reps. I care more about how hard the set is than hitting some huge rep number, so I would rather do a hard set of 6 to 8 than drag a set out forever.

I usually train around 1 rep in reserve, and sometimes I go all the way to failure. I know a set was hard enough when I feel like I could not do another clean rep, or maybe had one rep left at most. That is the kind of intensity I personally try to bring to most of my working sets.

What Recovery Looks Like for Me

I do not cook all the time, but when I have time I like making things like ground beef, chicken, or steak. Cooking is kind of fun, and I like knowing exactly what is going into my food instead of guessing. The steak photo here was actually from a cooking competition I had with a friend after the gym back in Houston. I was trying to prove I was the better cook, but I ended up losing.

For recovery, I try to get around 7 to 8 hours of sleep and I do not train every single day. Rest days give me time to recover from hard workouts, and I would rather come back ready to train hard than force myself into the gym every day just to say I went.

The Cardio I Actually Do

I went to Triathlon Club with a couple friends and we ran more than five miles around Forest Park. It was actually a really good time, and since I was cutting at the time, I also liked getting some extra activity in while hanging out with friends.

My actual secret cheat code is incline walking though. I like it more than long-distance running because it is less tiring and feels way more sustainable. I usually do 3 speed, 15 incline, for 30 minutes, and so far it has worked pretty well for me while trying to lose weight.

When the Gym Isn't the Priority

Sometimes the gym just is not the top priority. During exams, recruiting, or other activities, I have had days where I needed to leave early, skip exercises, or wait forever for a machine. Sumers around 6:00 PM can be insane. I would rather get a shorter workout in than feel like every session has to be two hours and perfectly follow my program.

I have had to improvise while traveling too. When I flew out for interviews, I still used the hotel gym even if the exercise selection was weird. During WashU Bear Trek in Chicago, I only had free weights and cables, but I was still able to get a quick lift in. For me, making the effort matters more than making every workout perfect.

I Stopped Comparing Myself to Fitness Influencers

When I first started going to the gym, I compared myself to a bunch of fitness influencers and tried to model myself after them. After a while, I realized that comparison was pretty unrealistic. Some people have amazing genetics, build their entire lifestyle around the gym, and some physiques may also involve steroids or other performance-enhancing drugs.

I still look at fitness influencers for inspiration, but I do not get disappointed anymore when I look different from them. I get way more satisfaction from looking at my own progress and knowing that I am better than I was yesterday.

Why I Like Training With Friends

One of my favorite parts of lifting is doing it with friends. When I came back to Houston from WashU, I got to see some of my hometown friends for the first time in months. We lifted together, compared how our strength matched up, and encouraged each other during the workout.

Afterward, we went out for all-you-can-eat hotpot and Korean barbecue. It was honestly just a really fun day. I got a productive workout in, but it also felt like meaningful time with friends I had not seen in a while.
